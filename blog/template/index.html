Inside a research story:
from question to evidence

Start with the problem, explain one central idea, and show what the experiment actually taught you.

01 / Motivation

The problem

Open with a concrete situation: what should a robot do, and where does the current approach fall short? Give readers enough context to understand the difficulty before introducing terminology.

Use one example throughout the article so the reader can connect the motivation, method, and final result.

02 / Insight

The key idea

Describe the central contribution in one or two sentences. State what changes in the system and why that change could address the problem.

Starting point

Explain the existing approach and the specific assumption that limits it.

Proposed approach

Explain the new mechanism and how it changes behavior in the same situation.

03 / Method

How it works

  1. Define the input

    Specify what the system observes: images, language, state, or demonstrations.

  2. Explain the mechanism

    Walk through the main transformation. Pair this explanation with a diagram or a small example.

  3. Connect to action

    Explain the output and how it determines the robot’s next decision.

  4. Evaluate the behavior

    Define what counts as success and which baselines make the comparison meaningful.

Implementation details & experimental setup

Place model settings, dataset definitions, training configuration, equations, and reproducibility notes here. Keep the main explanation readable without requiring this section.

04 / Evidence

Results & observations

Lead with the most informative result. Add a rollout video, comparison figure, or measured table, and explain what it supports. Report the evaluation conditions and uncertainty alongside any quantitative claim.

Observation → Interpretation → Caveat
Separate what was measured from what you think explains it. Include a failure case when it reveals the boundary of the approach.
Additional experiments

Add ablations, per-task breakdowns, and supplementary rollouts here. Label axes, units, and evaluation settings.

05 / Discussion

Limitations & next steps

Describe where the method fails, what remains untested, and which assumptions may not transfer. Tie each next step to an observed limitation.

06 / Closing thought

What to take away

Return to the opening question. Explain what the reader now knows and the next research question it raises.

References & resources

Add the paper, code, datasets, and related work here when writing a real article. Use descriptive link labels and credit collaborators and visual sources.
